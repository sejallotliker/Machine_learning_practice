Brief information about what are superconductors: (For those who are new to superconductivity phenomena)

The resistance of a normal metal gradually decreases as the temperature is lowered and levels off at very low temperatures. Many metals, however, undergo a phase transition to the superconducting state, whereby these metals have zero resistance below some temperature, Tc, the superconducting transition temperature. 

The phase transition to a superconducting phase effect was discovered by Kamerlingh Onnes in 1911, shortly after he had for the first time liquefied
helium (boiling point = 4.2 K). Some typical Tc’s for elemental superconductors are 9.25 K for Niobium (Nb), 7.2 K for lead (Pb), 1.1 K for Al, and even silicon is superconducting under pressure at 7.1 K!

In 1986s, the high Tc superconductivity in transition metal cuprate compounds was discovered , with Tc values far exceeding the previous record of 23.2 K, and by 1987 Tc values of > 120 K were reported, ushering in a new period of high research activity on high Tc superconducting materials.
